About this tool

A calculator that scales from homework to graphing and tables

Graphing calculators like the TI-84 Plus are the standard in most algebra, trig and calculus classrooms, but they aren't always within reach. This TI-84 style calculator covers three sides of that: a full scientific keypad for everyday calculations, a Graph tab that plots up to three functions at once with adjustable window bounds and zoom, and a Table tab that lists x/y values the same way the physical device's TABLE screen does.

The 2nd key works the way it does on the real calculator — press it once and sin, cos, tan, log and ln swap to their inverse or exponential counterparts, so one key does two jobs instead of cluttering the keypad. ANS recalls your last result and STO→ lets you save a value into memory for later in the same session. The keypad also includes factorial, absolute value, cube and cube root, nCr and nPr for combinatorics, and GCD/LCM for whole numbers.

Every calculation you run is kept in a short session history. Use the Back/Up and Next/Down buttons above the keypad to step through older and newer entries without retyping them, and use the matching Trace and Table navigation buttons to move through a graphed curve or a table of values one step at a time.

This is an independent, fan-built tool inspired by the classic graphing calculator layout for familiarity. It is not made by, affiliated with, or endorsed by Texas Instruments.

User guide

How to use the calculator

Works with mouse, touch, or your keyboard.

1

Pick a tab

Use Calculator for arithmetic and scientific functions, Graph to plot functions, or Table to list x/y values.

2

Try 2nd functions

Press 2nd, then sin, cos, tan, log or ln to reach their inverse/exponential counterpart.

3

Graph up to 3 functions

Type Y1, Y2 and/or Y3 in terms of x, tick the ones you want visible, set your window, then tap Graph.

4

Trace, scroll, and page

Click the graph or use the Trace buttons to read coordinates; use Back/Up and Next/Down to browse calculation history or table rows.

Who this is for

Solve equations, check trig values in degrees or radians, and simplify expressions with parentheses and exponents.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is this TI-84 style calculator free?

Yes, it's completely free to use in your browser, with no download, install or sign-up required.

Does it graph functions?

Yes. Switch to the Graph tab, type up to three expressions (Y1, Y2, Y3) in terms of x, and it plots them instantly in different colors with adjustable window bounds, zoom in, zoom out, a standard zoom reset, and left/right trace buttons.

Can it graph more than one function at a time?

Yes. The Graph tab supports three independent functions, Y1, Y2 and Y3, each with its own color and an on/off checkbox, so you can compare curves the same way you would on a physical graphing calculator.

Does it have a table of values like the TI-84?

Yes. The Table tab lists x and y values for your active graph functions starting at a chosen TblStart with a chosen step size. Up and down buttons scroll one row at a time, and back and next buttons page through a full screen of rows.

Does it support factorial, permutations and combinations?

Yes. The keypad includes factorial, nCr for combinations, nPr for permutations, absolute value, cube and cube root, and GCD/LCM for whole numbers.

Can I recall previous calculations?

Yes. Every calculation is saved to a session history. Use the back and up buttons to step to older entries and the next and down buttons to step forward again, similar to pressing the entry-recall key on a physical calculator.

What functions does the calculator support?

Standard arithmetic, parentheses, powers, roots, sin/cos/tan and their inverses, log and ln with their 2nd-function inverses, EE scientific notation, ANS and STO memory, percent, factorial, permutations and combinations, GCD and LCM, a random number generator, multi-function graphing with trace, and a table of values.

How does the 2nd key work?

Pressing 2nd swaps several keys to their alternate function, just like a physical graphing calculator: sin becomes sin inverse, log becomes 10 to the power of x, and so on. Press 2nd again to switch back.

Is this an official Texas Instruments product?

No. This is an independent, fan-made calculator with a TI-84 style layout for familiarity. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a product of Texas Instruments.

Can I use this for exams?

Check with your instructor or test administrator first. Many exams require a physical calculator, and an online tool like this is best suited for homework, practice and everyday calculations.
