About this tool

A calculator that scales from homework to graphing

Graphing calculators like the TI-84 Plus are the standard in most algebra, trig and calculus classrooms, but they aren't always within reach. This TI-84 style calculator covers both sides of that: a full scientific keypad for everyday calculations, and a dedicated Graph tab for plotting functions with adjustable window bounds and zoom, the same way you would on the physical device.

The 2nd key works the way it does on the real calculator — press it once and sin, cos, tan, log and ln swap to their inverse or exponential counterparts, so one key does two jobs instead of cluttering the keypad. ANS recalls your last result and STO→ lets you save a value into memory for later in the same session.

This is an independent, fan-built tool inspired by the classic graphing calculator layout for familiarity. It is not made by, affiliated with, or endorsed by Texas Instruments.

User guide

How to use the calculator

Works with mouse, touch, or your keyboard.

1

Pick a tab

Use Calculator for arithmetic and scientific functions, or Graph to plot a function.

2

Try 2nd functions

Press 2nd, then sin, cos, tan, log or ln to reach their inverse/exponential counterpart.

3

Graph an expression

Type a function in terms of x, set your window, then tap Graph. Zoom in/out anytime.

4

Trace the curve

Click or tap anywhere on the plotted graph to read the nearest (x, y) coordinate.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is this TI-84 style calculator free?

Yes, it's completely free to use in your browser, with no download, install or sign-up required.

Does it graph functions?

Yes. Switch to the Graph tab, type an expression in terms of x, and it plots instantly with adjustable window bounds, zoom in, zoom out, and a standard zoom reset.

What functions does the calculator support?

Standard arithmetic, parentheses, powers, roots, sin/cos/tan and their inverses, log and ln with their 2nd-function inverses, EE scientific notation, ANS and STO memory, percent, and function graphing with zoom.

How does the 2nd key work?

Pressing 2nd swaps several keys to their alternate function, just like a physical graphing calculator: sin becomes sin inverse, log becomes 10 to the power of x, and so on. Press 2nd again to switch back.

Is this an official Texas Instruments product?

No. This is an independent, fan-made calculator with a TI-84 style layout for familiarity. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a product of Texas Instruments.

Can I use this for exams?

Check with your instructor or test administrator first. Many exams require a physical calculator, and an online tool like this is best suited for homework, practice and everyday calculations.
